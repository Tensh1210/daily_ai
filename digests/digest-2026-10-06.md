## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **ExpHarness: Model-Agnostic Experience Learning through a Trainable Harness** — Tao Feng, …, Jiaxuan You · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30712
  ExpHarness puts learning into the agent harness instead of the model: trajectories are distilled into reusable skills and failure lessons inside a self-evolving experience graph. A lightweight retrieval copilot, trained with RL on a with/without-experience utility reward, decides per task how broadly to explore the graph and whether to favor historically useful over merely similar experiences. Executors stay frozen and swappable. **Why it matters:** Experience reuse that transfers across frozen/replaceable LLMs — fits closed-model agent stacks.
- **COPEX: Benchmarking LLM Robustness to Adversarial Context Across Model Context Protocol Layers** — Nahom Birhan et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04378
  COPEX isolates the model as an MCP client by fixing the agent stack and varying only the tool-selecting model. It covers 25 attack types (125 scenarios) across four entry surfaces: model/agent, client, server/tool and transport. Across nine models and 3,375 trials mean attack success is 64.4%; combined input and context scanning cuts it by 49.6%. **Why it matters:** Hard numbers on how exposed MCP clients are, and which defenses actually help.
- **CIPO: Counterfactual Imagination Policy Optimization for Adaptive Tool Granularity Selection** — Yu Li et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04991
  When atomic tools and composite "skills" coexist, choosing between them becomes a policy problem. CIPO mines executable skills from successful tool-use trajectories under a budget and trains the granularity decision with counterfactual branch rollouts comparing atomic vs. skill choices from the same state. **Why it matters:** Principled way to decide when an agent should call a skill vs. low-level tools.
- **Understanding and Mitigating Hallucination Escape in Tool-Using LLM Agents** — Peigui Qi et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04409
  Tool-hallucination mitigations reduce errors on the tool configuration they were tuned on but increase them on others — "hallucination escape". The authors trace this to conflicts between a model's intrinsic tool-use tendencies and the current tool set, and propose EscapeGuard, a training-free method with conflict-aware gating and configuration-derived attention enhancement. **Why it matters:** Warns that tool-calling fixes can silently regress when your tool set changes.
- **RAISED: Self-Distillation for Robustness to Prompt Injection in LLM Agents** — Mohamed Dhouib, …, Elie Bursztein · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06401
  Training-based prompt-injection defenses drift the output distribution and make agents skip legitimate steps suggested by tool outputs. RAISED has the model generate its own tool-use scenarios (emphasizing cases where acting on tool output is legitimate) and then self-distills for attack invariance. **Why it matters:** Injection robustness without the usual utility tax on benign tool use.
- **Plant, Persist, Trigger: Sleeper Attack on Large Language Model Agents** — Yongxiang Li et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28201
  Adversarial content from tool data, webpages or MCP context can persist in agent state, stay dormant, and later be triggered by a benign user query. The authors formalize this "Sleeper Attack" and release a 1,896-instance benchmark spanning six harmful outcomes, three strategies and three state targets: session context, memory and reusable skills. **Why it matters:** Memory and skill libraries are a persistent attack surface, not just single-turn context.
- **AIProver: Agentic Auto-Formalization of Mathematical Research via Certificate-Driven Evolving Harness** — Prithwish Jana et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05367
  AIProver jointly post-trains a 119B open-weight model and evolves its tool-calling harness (HarnessEvolve) for proof auto-formalization into Lean. Verifiers check type correctness, proof completeness and semantic fidelity, returning diagnostic certificates that drive fine-tuning and alternating RL. **Why it matters:** Co-evolving model + harness on open weights instead of relying on costly frontier models.
- **AECG: Asymmetric Experience Consolidation and Governance In Multi-Agent Systems** — Ao Tian et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05176
  Multi-agent memory suffers from pollution (stale or spuriously successful procedures) and "scope collapse", where knowledge escapes the coordination level where it worked. AECG turns memory into a reliability-governance loop that preserves coordination scope and uses multi-scale, confidence-aware consolidation. **Why it matters:** Addresses why shared agent memory degrades over time in multi-agent setups.
- **MLLMs Fail to Refuse when Using Tools Agentically** — Rikiya Takehi et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03938
  Across three safety benchmarks, all tested open and closed multimodal LLMs refuse harmful requests significantly less when operating with tools (zoom, tagging) than without, with relative refusal-failure rates rising up to 68.7%. Analysis of 100k+ responses suggests two causes. **Why it matters:** Safety tuning does not carry over cleanly into agentic tool-use mode.
- **TeleTune: Evolving Agent Skills From Offline Telemetry** — Justin Chih-Yao Chen et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05437
  TeleTune learns a textual skill library for computer-use agents from user telemetry logs that lack goals, cannot be replayed, and interleave tasks. It proposes library edits from action-prediction errors and keeps only edits that improve held-out action prediction; learned workflows also drive demonstration retrieval. **Why it matters:** Scalable route to agent skills from existing product usage logs.
- **TrajLong: Co-Designing Agentic and Long-Context Supervision for Mid-Training** — Miao Peng et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04973
  TrajLong compiles agent trajectories into dense long-context training tasks targeting evidence grounding, cross-evidence aggregation and temporal state maintenance. Mid-training Qwen3-14B-Base and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base on this data plus SFT yields gains on 6 long-context and 12 agent benchmarks. **Why it matters:** Recipe for using agent traces to build long-context skills in mid-training.
- **From Memory to Guide: Spatio-Temporal Composer for Procedural Coding Memory** — Zhixuan Tan et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04868
  Instead of pasting retrieved skills into prompts, the Composer compiles them into bounded "Runtime Guides" aligned with current environment constraints (spatial) and with an explicit applicable lifecycle (temporal). Evaluated on 13 long-horizon software-engineering tasks without parameter updates. **Why it matters:** Treats skill memory as active policy shaping, not just context stuffing.
- **Training Agents to Evolve with Their Harness: TaoLive Digital Avatar Agent Technical Report** — TaoLive AIGC LLM Team · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15763
  Harness-Aware Training (HAT) trains compact models to tolerate changing Skills, Hooks, prompts and tool schemas. Its Harness-State Augmentation perturbs these during SFT, followed by on-policy distillation and HSA-RL for robustness. **Why it matters:** Lets small, low-latency models keep up with fast-iterating production harnesses.
- **The Cost of a Hop: Benchmarking NLIP and A2A** — Ranjan Sinha et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04053
  A controlled latency comparison of the NLIP and A2A agent protocols, decomposed into message creation, connection and send. NLIP is 4–9.6x faster for lightweight coordination, almost entirely due to connection setup; end-to-end with LLM inference the two are near parity, and A2A connection caching narrows the gap. **Why it matters:** Protocol overhead matters for chatty multi-agent coordination, not for LLM-bound pipelines.
- **SEIS: Self-Evolving Inference Systems** — Zhen Xu et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04646
  SEIS applies agentic self-evolution to rewrite the whole mini-sglang serving engine end-to-end across iterative sessions. Serving Qwen3-0.6B on H100, the result reaches 3.27x the original throughput and beats vLLM, TensorRT-LLM and SGLang on single-request workloads, with correctness checked numerically and on downstream tasks. **Why it matters:** Coding agents optimizing entire systems, not just isolated kernels.

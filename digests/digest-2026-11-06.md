## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **MCPHallu: Benchmarking Reasoning, Execution, and Memory Hallucinations in MCP Agents** — Jiang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07689
  MCPHallu has 358 tasks on 36 production MCP servers, each built with a structural trigger for one failure subtype: Branch Collapse, Unreachable Goal, Tool Misuse or Context Forgetting. Across 14 frontier agents and 5,012 trajectories the average score is 0.642. Unreachable Goal is worst at 0.458 with 23.2% of runs passing, because agents keep calling tools or claim success instead of recognizing an infeasible goal. A model's rank moves 6.9 of 13 positions on average across subtypes. **Why it matters:** aggregate success hides which failure you get; agents that cannot say "this is impossible" are a concrete design target.
- **The Last Mile to the Real World: Agent-Native Primitives as Reusable Building Blocks for Solving Real-World Tasks** — Jin et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07461
  Agent-native primitives attach a resident LLM to a resource so it can apply that resource in context, and HEELO composes them into a per-task harness, verifying and revising from environment evidence. The authors report Terminal-Bench 4.0 rising from 37.3% under Codex to 55.5% on the same backbone. On their own 46-task platform, Claude Code and Codex complete 9 tasks each alone, and 39 and 35 through HEELO's MCP server. **Why it matters:** it locates agent failure in applying a resource rather than reaching it, though the largest gains come from the authors' own platform.
- **Beyond Instruction Override: Benchmarking Indirect Prompt Injection with Task-Tampering Attacks** — Zhang et al. (Amazon Science repo) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07640
  This work defines injection by authority: planted content is an attack if it tries to influence a decision its source is not authorized to make, even without changing the task. The benchmark has 90 validated cases in three tool-use environments. Detection-and-removal cuts objective attacks to 2.4% but leaves observation and state attacks at 9.5% and 12.1%. A tool-policy defense allows 15.4% success against 17.0% with no defense. **Why it matters:** defenses evaluated only on task-redirection attacks can overestimate their coverage.
- **Don't Prompt Your Agents. Equip Them: Skillware as a Modular Standard for Sovereign, Auditable, and Deterministic Capability Execution for Logical Systems** — Peilivanidis · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07652
  Skillware replaces text-based skills with versioned bundles containing a typed JSON input contract, deterministic Python, task directives, datasets and unit tests. Adapters expose them as native tool declarations for any model. Across 23 models and 368 conditions, the author reports 3B to 8B local models with Skillware outperforming unequipped frontier models on multi-step workflows, with up to 84.8% fewer tokens and 97.3% lower inference cost. **Why it matters:** a direct, author-reported argument for deterministic code over markdown skills, relevant to anyone designing agent tooling.
- **Persistent Retrieval for Conversational Agents: A Study of Retrieval Efficiency** — Ravula et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07899
  The paper compares skill-based retrieval in the main agent, a non-persistent subagent that writes findings, and a persistent subagent that keeps retrieval history and returns evidence directly. Against skill-based retrieval, the persistent design uses 43.3% fewer tokens and costs 45.0% less, and it is 9.1% faster. Against the non-persistent subagent, it saves 15.6 seconds per question but uses 65.1% more tokens and costs 29.1% more. **Why it matters:** gives measured trade-offs for a common subagent design decision.
- **Harness Updating Is Not Harness Benefit: Disentangling Evolution Capabilities in Self-Evolving LLM Agents** — Lin et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30621
  The authors separate two abilities: producing useful harness updates, and benefiting from them. Update quality is flat across capability tiers, with Qwen3.5-9B's updates yielding gains comparable to Claude Opus 4.6's. Benefit is non-monotonic: mid-tier models gain most, and weak models often fail to activate or follow the artifacts. **Why it matters:** the evolver can be cheap; spend the capability budget on the task-solving agent.
- **ASTRA-bench: Evaluating Tool-Use Agent Reasoning and Action Planning with Personal User Context** — Xiu et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01357
  ASTRA-bench combines time-evolving personal context, an interactive toolbox and complex user intents across 2,413 scenarios. Models including Claude-4.5-Opus and DeepSeek-V3.2 degrade significantly at high complexity. Argument generation is the main bottleneck. **Why it matters:** points to tool arguments grounded in messy personal context as the weak link.
- **ARMs: Adaptive Red-Teaming Agent against Multimodal Models with Plug-and-Play Attacks** — Chen et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02677
  ARMs is an adaptive red-teaming agent for vision-language models with 11 new multimodal attack strategies and 17 algorithms integrated via MCP. It exceeds baselines by an average of 52.1% on attack success and surpasses 90% on Claude-4-Sonnet. Its 30K-instance ARMs-Bench dataset also improves robustness through safety fine-tuning. **Why it matters:** shows MCP used as an integration layer for attack tooling, and provides a dataset for hardening.
- **The Two-Ledger Model: Transaction-Grain Cost Accounting for Multi-Agent AI Systems Across Clouds, Models, and Systems of Record** — Acharya · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06989
  Billing data cannot answer what one order or claim cost in AI, because providers never see business transaction boundaries. The proposal joins a FOCUS settlement ledger to an invocation-grain attribution ledger built on OpenTelemetry GenAI, correlated through trace context, A2A/MCP metadata, business keys and probabilistic matching. In simulation, cost coverage stays above 99% as trace propagation falls to zero, and mid-flight gating cuts p99 transaction cost by up to 61%. **Why it matters:** a concrete schema for per-transaction agent cost, validated by simulation rather than production data.
- **SKIM: Pruning Large Language Model Agents via Selective Knowledge Informed Masking** — Choi et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07558
  Under grammar-constrained generation, logits matter most at the few positions that decide an agent's action. SKIM splits tokens into context, decision and format roles to guide both pruning and on-policy distillation. It improves accuracy over baselines on tool-calling benchmarks, most clearly at high sparsity. **Why it matters:** a compression approach designed for structured tool-calling agents.
- **Towards Foundation Decision Models: Drex** — Dhankhar et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07854
  Drex is a family of four open decision models that return a probability distribution over user-supplied options, without autoregressive decoding. On Decision Bench v4, Max ties GPT-6 Sol and trails Claude Opus 5.5 by 1.6 points. It answers a typical request in 183 ms, against about 2.0 s and 3.5 s respectively, and its outputs stay stable under option reordering. **Why it matters:** open models for routing and classification steps where chain-of-thought latency and inconsistency hurt.
- **CacheGraft: Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Latent-Space Document Enrichment** — Lakshman et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07393
  CacheGraft encodes passages offline into fused KV caches using a 4B sharer and a 0.6B receiver, then injects them into the small model at query time without the large one. It beats the text-only baseline in 29 of 30 comparisons, 17 of them statistically significant, across five QA benchmarks. **Why it matters:** a possible way to get better RAG quality on compute-constrained deployments.
- **TileQuant: Tile as the Precision-Allocation Unit for LLM Mixed-Precision Post-Training Quantization** — Zhang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07217
  TileQuant assigns precision to hardware-aligned 2D weight tiles rather than columns, keeping top-scoring tiles at 16-bit and the rest at 8-bit. It is training-free and uses offline calibration. It reaches 2.66x the original model's generation throughput while preserving quality across tasks including reasoning and long-context retrieval. **Why it matters:** a finer precision unit that aligns with how hardware executes, for cheaper local inference.
- **The Power of Meta Access in the Software of the Future --- Demonstrated on LLM Chat UIs and Chat AI** — Kunkel & Doosthosseini · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08428
  Meta access gives an assistant a structured, permissioned view of its own interface, on a ladder from feature tutoring to full orchestration. The Meta Chat prototype builds bounded context from summaries and settings and supports confirmed, reversible changes. Because harnesses can change their own settings, the authors argue this needs a separate control-plane boundary. **Why it matters:** a useful framing for permissions when agents can edit the tools that host them.
- **DelegateEval: Evaluating Platforms for Personal External Service Agents** — Li et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07508
  DelegateEval proposes eight dimensions for judging platforms where agents serve an individual's external clients, including delivery quality, expertise inheritance and governance. Only the framework and an experimental design are presented. Datasets, environments and cross-model results remain to be developed. **Why it matters:** an early framing for agents as a personal service business, not yet empirically validated.

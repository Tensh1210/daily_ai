## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **Agent Tools Orchestration Leaks More: Dataset, Benchmark, and Mitigation** — Qiao, Liu, Yang, Zhou, Hu (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16310
  The paper formalizes Tools Orchestration Privacy Risk (TOP-R): individually harmless tool returns that compose into a sensitive conclusion. It defines three conditions (conclusion sensitivity, single-source non-inferability, compositional inferability) and builds TOP-Bench, a 1,000-instance benchmark under a two-stage tool-use protocol. Across six agents, task completion averaged 98.0% but leakage 88.6%; with native reasoning enabled, four models leaked in ~81% of final responses and ~82% of reasoning traces. Prompt-only safeguards only partially close the gap. **Why it matters:** Per-tool permission checks are structurally insufficient — the leak lives in the composition, which is exactly what nobody audits.

- **UniToolCall: Unifying Tool-Use Representation, Data, and Evaluation for LLM Agents** — Liang, Chen, Ge et al. (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11557
  UniToolCall standardizes the whole tool-learning pipeline: toolset construction, dataset generation, and evaluation. It curates 22k+ tools and 390k+ training instances by combining 10 standardized public datasets with structurally controlled synthetic trajectories. It explicitly models single-hop vs multi-hop and single-turn vs multi-turn interaction patterns rather than treating all tool calls alike. **Why it matters:** Tool-use benchmarks are mutually incompatible today; a shared representation is the precondition for comparing agent tool competence at all.

- **How Fast Do Agents Rot? An Empirical Study of Long-Horizon Degradation in LLM Agents** — Shubhra Mittal (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01660
  A controlled study across nine models (six open, 1.2B–671B, plus three deployed proprietary systems), four task families, five horizons, and three context regimes. Task success follows a geometric law governed by a single per-step reliability parameter that rises with model scale but does not disappear. The core claim: the benchmark/production gap is largely an artifact of task horizon, since benchmarks cluster at short-to-medium horizons while production demands an order of magnitude more dependent steps. **Why it matters:** Gives a one-number model for predicting whether your 40-step workflow will hold up — far more actionable than another leaderboard percentage.

- **Repo-To-Skill: Distilling GitHub Repositories Into AI4AI Skills** — Chen, Hu, Qian et al. (HF Papers) · https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02749
  Argues that ML research agents lack an "operational knowledge" layer — the know-how separating knowing a method from making it work — which lives in repos and papers in forms too large to load per task. DisCo is a skill-powered research agent that distills that knowledge into compact, verified skills, both task-agnostic (condensing widely used repositories) and task-specific. Skills are then reused across tasks instead of rediscovered on every run. **Why it matters:** Direct validation of the skills/context-packaging pattern that agent harnesses are converging on.

- **SkillGLoW: Procedural-Family Skill Consolidation for Self-Improving Agents** — Yan, Zhang, Du, Zhou (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02217
  Identifies two opposite failure modes in self-improving agents: a single global skill document collapses into generic advice, while a flat per-task skill pool inflates and stays bound to the instance that wrote it. SkillGLoW proposes the solving procedure shared by a cluster of related tasks as the right unit of reuse. Local skills are aggregated into procedural families and compressed into de-instantiated global priors; instance detail is regenerated per task rather than stored. **Why it matters:** A concrete answer to skill-library bloat, the main thing that breaks long-running agent memory.

- **APEx: Distillation of Agent Procedural Experience for Adaptive Deep Research QA** — Ding, Sun, Zhang, Zhang, Liu (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02253
  APEx organizes interaction history into instance-level trajectory memories plus category-level procedural skills, coupled through a closed loop of Executor, Distiller, and Planner. The three modules are optimized via a three-stage alternating GRPO paradigm, making skill distillation reward-guided rather than fixed-prompt generation. It targets the gap where retrieved traces are too verbose to act on and distilled skills stay decoupled from policy adaptation. **Why it matters:** Makes experience reuse a trained component instead of a prompt-engineering afterthought.

- **Belief-Calibrated Optimization: An Explicit World Model for Agentic Optimization** — Chen, Tian, Dabas et al. (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01861
  When a coding agent is used as an optimizer over a scaffold, each edit encodes an implicit belief about how the environment will respond — a belief that evaporates between calls, so later rounds see only scores and traces. BCO writes that belief down as a persistent in-context document and continually revises it as new candidates are evaluated. Later iterations then inherit the accumulated causal model. **Why it matters:** Cheap, harness-level fix for the "agent re-learns the same lesson every iteration" problem.

- **Imagine Before Retrieval: Prospective Skill Retrieval for LLM Agents** — Liu, Yang, Xiao, Yang, Peng (arXiv cs.IR) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01642
  Names Query–Skill Misalignment (QSM): task queries are objective-oriented while skills are procedural-oriented, so semantic-similarity retrieval over a skill gallery systematically misses the right skill. The paper argues matching in the original space is close to impossible under QSM, and instead imagines the prospective procedure first, then retrieves against that. **Why it matters:** Explains why naive vector search over a skill library underperforms, and points at the fix.

- **OmegaUse-SOP: SOP Engineering for Professional Computer Use from Human Demonstrations** — Xiao, An, Yang et al. (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02149
  A human-in-the-loop system that converts recorded demonstrations of professional software use into reusable SOP skills for GUI agents. It targets domain-specific procedures that general computer-use benchmarks miss: implicit domain knowledge, software-specific conventions, and task-level verification requirements. The authors frame "SOP Engineering" as an iterative discipline analogous to prompt engineering. **Why it matters:** Demonstration-to-skill is the realistic onboarding path for GUI agents in enterprise software that has no training data.

- **OptSkills: Learning Generalizable Optimization Skills from Problem Archetypes via Cluster-Based Distillation** — Yang, Zhao, Ma, Lu, Wang, Qian (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29829
  LLM-based optimization modeling generalizes poorly: it is sensitive to superficial narrative variation and reuses experience only at the case level. OptSkills clusters problems by underlying archetype rather than surface narrative, explores diverse modeling paradigms and solver configurations within each cluster, then distills successful trajectories into reusable workflows. **Why it matters:** Archetype-level clustering is a transferable recipe for any domain where agents keep re-solving structurally identical problems.

- **NeoMME: A Single-Tower Multimodal-Native Multilingual Foundation Encoder** — Aurélien Lac, Tony Wu (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01657
  260M and 800M bidirectional encoders that process multilingual text and raw image patches in one Transformer, pretrained from scratch with a masked discrete-diffusion text objective conditioned on visible image patches. Unlike ColPali-style retrievers that repurpose a generative VLM, NeoMME avoids carrying VLM parameter and compute overhead for a non-generative task. Context is 16,384 tokens — enough to encode up to two 4K UHD images. **Why it matters:** Document retrieval at a fraction of VLM cost, relevant to anyone paying VLM prices for embeddings.

- **WhiFlash: Accelerating Speculative Decoding with Token-Level Cross-Paradigm Routing** — Kwon, Williams, Li, Kouris, Venieris (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07710
  The authors find drafting accuracy fluctuates sharply within a single sequence, so static speculative-decoding paradigms and coarse-grained routing leave throughput unrealised. WhiFlash unifies autoregressive drafting (better for reasoning) and diffusion-based parallel drafting (better for structured output) under one token-level routing controller. It is framed explicitly around complex agentic workloads where both output types interleave. **Why it matters:** Agent traces mix prose reasoning and structured tool JSON — exactly the case static drafters handle worst.

- **Multi-Agent RAG for Efficient Cloud Knowledge Base Search in a Telecom SNOC** — Saragadam, Sharma, Routray (arXiv cs.IR) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01618
  Athena is a fully offline multi-agent RAG framework deployed in Vodafone Idea's service and network operations center over SOPs, vendor manuals, incident reports, and configuration guides. It combines E5-Large-V2 dense retrieval, BM25 sparse retrieval, and knowledge-graph expansion inside a LangGraph orchestration framework. The constraints are production-realistic: offline operation, incident-time latency, heterogeneous document types. **Why it matters:** A rare deployed-and-measured multi-agent RAG report rather than a benchmark-only design.

- **LLMs for Telecom Root Cause Analysis: A Structured Reasoning Framework for Evidence-Grounded Diagnosis** — Zhou, Kulkarni, Chen, Xin et al. (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02805
  Reviews telecom RCA's evolution from rule-based and ML approaches to LLM-enabled techniques, covering structured reasoning, retrieval-augmented knowledge grounding, agentic orchestration, and verifiable reasoning. It then proposes a structured reasoning framework to counter the failure modes of vanilla LLMs on 5G/6G RCA: hallucination, unstable reasoning, and poor alignment with structured network evidence. **Why it matters:** The evidence-grounding discipline generalizes to any agent diagnosing a system from cross-layer telemetry.

- **RideSkill: A Hierarchical Algorithm for Generalized Ride Sharing with LLM-Driven Automatic Evolution** — Zhao, Li, Tong, Yuan (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02250
  Multi-agent RL for ride-sharing suffers from limited generalization across scenarios, low transferability across platform objectives, and training difficulty at scale. RideSkill uses a hierarchical algorithm with LLM-driven automatic evolution rather than putting the LLM in the decision loop directly. The split keeps combinatorial assignment in a scalable algorithm while the LLM evolves the strategy layer. **Why it matters:** A good template for LLM-as-algorithm-designer instead of LLM-as-controller in latency-critical operations.

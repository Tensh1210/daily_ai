## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **APDMem: Agent-Controlled Progressive Disclosure for Query-Adaptive Long-Term Memory** — Chin-Lun Fu et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02472
  APDMem stores conversation history as four layers (thematic summaries, key facts, turn-level evidence notes, raw messages), and an agent controller reads top-down, going deeper only when the query needs it. Simple queries stop early, while temporal, multi-hop or exact-evidence queries trigger deeper inspection. A note synthesizer consolidates facts, orders events and flags contradictions before the answer. **Why it matters:** Progressive-disclosure memory is a practical cost/fidelity pattern for long-lived personal agents.
- **Choosing Before Acting: Comparative Value Estimation for Long-Horizon Tool-Use Agents** — Yu Li et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02330
  CITA argues tool-use agents should estimate the long-horizon value of a candidate tool call before executing it. It trains a Comparative Inference Model from paired signals that mix observed tool behavior with scalable Bayesian supervision, giving comparative step-level credit without extra rollouts or LLM judges. **Why it matters:** Cheaper step-level credit assignment for long tool-call chains.
- **LPS-Bench: Benchmarking Safety Awareness of Computer-Use Agents in Long-Horizon Planning under Benign and Adversarial Scenarios** — Tianyu Chen et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03255
  LPS-Bench evaluates long-horizon planning safety of computer-use agents in MCP-style tool workflows, under both benign requests and adversarial steering. A multi-agent pipeline generates instructions, simulated toolkits and case-specific safety criteria (human-reviewed): 570 cases across 65 scenarios, 7 domains and 9 risk types. An LLM evaluator inspects tool choices and arguments across the full trace, not just the final outcome. **Why it matters:** Catches unsafe early decisions in MCP/tool agents that outcome-only evals miss.
- **VERSE: Verified Self-Evolving Optimizer for Agent Harnesses** — Zekai Wang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02616
  VERSE is an optimizer that evolves an agent's harness (prompts, tools, workflow) and also improves its own tools and procedures. A controlled study shows optimizer self-evolution only helps when edits are verified by execution, so VERSE tests draft edits, replays failures and perturbs suspect steps before submitting, tracking fixes and regressions across rounds. **Why it matters:** Execution-verified self-improvement is the key ingredient for automated harness tuning.
- **Threat-Preserving Representation Sensitivity in Agent-Security Benchmarks** — Neeraj Karamchandani et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03585
  Shows agent-security benchmark scores depend on surface representation: renaming threat-related tool names to neutral ones in Agent Security Bench raises attack success rate by ~12-13 points on GPT-5-mini and Claude Haiku 4.5, and the reverse on MCPTox lowers it. The proposed TPRS metric measures ASR shift while holding task, threat and evaluation fixed. **Why it matters:** ASR comparisons across agents/defenses may be measuring tool naming, not robustness.
- **Toward SLM-based agentic task-tool intent matching** — Chiara Troiani et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03213
  Proposes using small language models as per-call task-tool relevance classifiers that check whether each selected tool is a logical step toward the task's intent. This complements authorization (is the call allowed?) with an intent check (does the call make sense?), at low latency and on-prem. **Why it matters:** Cheap intent-level oversight for every tool call in multi-agent systems.
- **WebFovea: When the Model Is Right but the Click Is Wrong -- Reliable Round Trips for Vision-Based Web Agents on Live Websites** — Jiangang Han · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03036
  WebFovea, 2nd in the WebRetriever Challenge 2026 (57/100), shows that many live-website failures happen in the harness, not in model reasoning: parsing actions, applying them, reporting results and showing the right info. Example: a coordinate-space mismatch placed every click at 3/4 of its intended position. **Why it matters:** Harness engineering is a first-class driver of web-agent reliability.
- **Recoverability Has a Law: The ERR Measure for Tool-Augmented Agents** — Sri Vatsa Vuddanti et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22352
  Formalizes agent recovery after failed tool calls via Expected Recovery Regret (ERR) and derives a first-order law linking it to an observable Efficiency Score. Validated across five tool-use benchmarks, predicted regret matched Monte Carlo rollouts within 0.05. **Why it matters:** Gives a falsifiable, measurable way to compare agents' error recovery.
- **Coco: An Agentic Copilot for the Hardware--Software Co-Design Lifecycle** — Samuel Kushnir et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02376
  Coco is an agentic copilot deployed with TPU architects for ML model/accelerator co-design. Every simulation sweep is registered into a normalized relational schema so agents ground numbers in SQL queries rather than scraping files, backed by a tool library for setting up experiments, sweeping simulators and deriving insights. **Why it matters:** Real-world pattern: ground agents in SQL over fresh data where no pretraining knowledge exists.
- **MobiAgent: Dual-Loop Recursive Policy Self-Improvement for Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation** — Chenzhi Liu et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03476
  MobiAgent is a dual-loop agentic framework for long-horizon mobile manipulation. The inner loop uses VLM receding-horizon planning and visual reflection to compose atomic skills executed by flow-matching experts on a shared VLM backbone; the outer loop recursively self-improves the policy. **Why it matters:** Brings skill composition and self-improvement from LLM agents into robotics.
- **MOF-VERIFY: A Failure-Aware Agentic Harness for MOF Hypothesis Verification** — Donghyun Lee et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03056
  Builds a diagnostic benchmark for verifying MOF (metal-organic framework) hypotheses across structural grounding, synthesis conditions, evidence sufficiency and MLIP-based computation, evaluated closed-book, with retrieval and with oracle evidence. Diagnosed failure modes then guide MOF-Verify, a failure-aware agentic harness. **Why it matters:** Template for diagnosing where AI co-scientist pipelines fail before building the agent.
- **Decoupling Memory from Context: Structured Memory for Token-Efficient Test-Time Continual Learning** — Yehya Farhat et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02687
  Frames agent memory updates as an optimization procedure over the model's context, unifying context engineering and memory systems. Uses structured memory decoupled from the context window so experience carries forward without ever-growing token costs. **Why it matters:** Principled route to test-time continual learning without bloating context.
- **Tailoring the Quantization Space for 1-Bit KV Cache Compression** — Minsoo Cheong et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03027
  TaSQ makes 1-bit vector-quantized KV caches viable by tailoring the quantization space: query-guided channel weighting, cross-head normalization and covariance-aware channel grouping. The transforms are RoPE-compatible and merge into projection weights and codebooks, adding negligible serving overhead. **Why it matters:** Extreme KV compression directly cuts long-context inference memory.
- **Large Language Continuous Diffusion Models** — Zhihan Yang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02665
  Sigma is the first large-scale (3B/8B) continuous diffusion language model, denoising Gaussian-corrupted token embeddings along steerable low-dimensional ODE/SDE trajectories and warm-started from autoregressive weights. With classifier-free guidance and score temperature, it is competitive with discrete diffusion and AR baselines on GSM8K, Minerva, HumanEval and MBPP. **Why it matters:** Continuous diffusion LMs become a credible option for steerable parallel decoding.
- **CLIMB: Confidence-Guided Complementary Evidence for Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation** — Hang Gao et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03421
  CLIMB is a training-free inference-time framework for multimodal RAG: it builds a compact MMR-style complementary evidence pool, then refines answers with a relevance/specificity/alignment critic, accepting an update only when estimated confidence rises. **Why it matters:** Simple stopping rule reduces redundant retrieval in knowledge-heavy VQA.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **Orchard: An Open-Source Agentic Modeling Framework** — Baolin Peng, Wenlin Yao, Qianhui Wu, Hao Cheng et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15040
  Orchard is an open framework for scalable agentic modeling, built on Orchard Env — a Kubernetes-native environment service providing reusable sandbox-lifecycle primitives across task domains, harnesses, and training stages. On top of it the authors ship three recipes, including Orchard-SWE for software-engineering agents. Training contributions include credit-assignment SFT and Balanced Adaptive Rollout (BAR) for sparse-reward RL. **Why it matters:** Infrastructure for agent RL is the current bottleneck; an open Kubernetes-native sandbox layer lowers the cost of reproducing agent training runs.
- **Beacon: Knowing When and How to Perform Agentic Visual Reasoning** — Qixun Wang, Yang Shi, Letian Cheng, Zhuoran Zhang et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28595
  Beacon reframes agentic visual reasoning around two axes of tool use: Mode Adaptiveness (does the MLLM know when a tool is actually needed) and Tool Effect (does the tool genuinely extend capability). The goal is success rate, not elaborate reasoning traces, so unnecessary tool calls are penalized as overhead. **Why it matters:** Targets the dominant failure mode of tool-using multimodal agents — calling tools reflexively instead of only when text-only reasoning would fail.
- **Training Skills Like Parameters via Self-Supervised Semantic Diffusion** — Mo Li, Zixin Yin, Ting Cao, Yunxin Liu · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27557
  The paper treats textual "skills" as trainable objects for closed-source LLMs where weight access is impossible, using self-supervised semantic diffusion instead of SFT or RL. This avoids reliance on costly expert annotation or unreliable LLM-as-a-judge feedback that plagues agentic continual learning. Skills stay human-inspectable rather than baked into a checkpoint. **Why it matters:** A weight-free path to specializing frontier API models, with the learned artifact remaining auditable text.
- **GuideSkill: Evolving Executable LLM Agent Skills for Guideline-Grounded Clinical Reasoning** — Lang Cao, Yuhao Shen, Tianyang Luo, Simo Du et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26160
  GuideSkill compiles clinical practice guidelines into executable functions returning ordinal diagnostic-support scores, rather than retrieving guideline text. GuideSkill-Zero initializes from guidelines; GuideSkill-Evo refines skills and adds missing diagnoses from case–diagnosis pairs. At inference the LLM proposes a differential, grounds required features, and fuses its ranking with executed skill scores. **Why it matters:** Turns compliance documents into deterministic code the agent must execute, making high-stakes reasoning checkable instead of merely retrieved.
- **CaM-Wolf: Causal-Aware Multimodal Agents for Social Deduction Games** — Zheng Zhang, Nanjie Yao, Jiarui He, Deheng Ye et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26393
  CaM-Wolf is the first Werewolf-style social-deduction agent to integrate multimodal perception and generation, consuming video of other players rather than text transcripts alone. A causal-aware Reasoner trained with RL links observable behaviors to hidden roles. **Why it matters:** Moves multi-agent social reasoning benchmarks closer to the noisy, embodied signals humans actually use to deceive and detect deception.
- **LEDGERMIND: Provenance-Constrained Multimodal Agentic Reasoning with a Structured Evidence Ledger** — Enjun Du, Hange Zhou, Chenxu Du, Siyi Liu et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28374
  LEDGERMIND models a multimodal agent trajectory as a provenance-constrained state machine: tool outputs are normalized into a Structured Evidence Ledger, and downstream claims may cite only active ledger entries. Grounding is checked at entity and numeric level, and repairs are typed state transitions that cannot introduce unsourced content. **Why it matters:** Replaces final-answer accuracy with a check on whether the answer was actually derived from tool evidence — the difference between grounded and lucky.
- **BM25 Wins at Scale: A Scaling Study of Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigms** — Pengyu Wang, Benfeng Xu, Shaohan Wang, Xin Zeng et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26497
  A controlled study varies corpus size across 28 nested tiers spanning ~450x while holding questions, reader model, and judging protocol fixed. Results show a scale-dependent crossover rather than a single winner: a File-System Agent leads at the smallest tiers but costs 39x more query tokens at larger ones, while BM25 wins at scale. **Why it matters:** Argues that agentic search's advantage is a small-corpus artifact, and that cost-normalized evaluation flips many published RAG comparisons.
- **SpatialCLI: Learning to Reason With Spatial Tools, Then Without Them** — Yang Zhou, Zixuan Huang, Sunzhu Li, Zhuo Yang et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27703
  SpatialCLI closes the gap between VLMs that reason at task level but miss visual detail and specialist vision models that see detail but cannot decide. Three stages — Call (expose specialists as spatial tools), Learn (cold-start SFT plus RL), and progressive internalization — let the VLM eventually reason without the tools. **Why it matters:** A concrete recipe for distilling tool dependence away, cutting inference cost for embodied agents once the skill is internalized.
- **LayerRAG-Bench: A Cross-Layer Reliability Benchmark for Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation** — Musa Shams · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27353
  LayerRAG-Bench probes agentic RAG failures at the evidence, tool-contract, authorization, and session-state layers across 8 enterprise domains, 240 tasks, 9 fault scenarios, and 38,880 live records over nine OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini models. Schema normalization lifts schema-drift success from 0.000 to 0.913, but stale evidence, missing tool output, denied permissions, and wrong-session context are not recovered. Groundedness-only evaluation produces many false positives. **Why it matters:** Shows enterprise agent reliability fails on permissions and session state — layers that groundedness metrics are blind to.
- **Filesystem-Based Memory for LLM Agents: Organization, Evolution, and Sustainability** — Sizhe Zhou, Sheldon Yu, Hui Wei, Junda Wu et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26637
  Deployed agents increasingly keep long-term memory as a directory tree of markdown files managed through generic file tools, yet the assumptions behind that default are untested. The paper formalizes three roles around one memory filesystem — a management agent that integrates and organizes, a search agent that answers, and the memory itself — and studies whether organization survives accumulation, conflict, and staleness. **Why it matters:** First systematic evidence on whether the now-ubiquitous "memory as markdown files" pattern actually pays off as it grows.
- **SkillRise: Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Cross-Task Skill Evolution** — Zhiyuan Yao, Yuxin Chen, Zhengxi Lu, Zishan Xu et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26784
  SkillRise organizes related tasks into progressively harder sequences and trains a single policy that alternates between solving a task and curating an evolving skill document passed to the next task. Decoupled credit assignment separates supervision for solving from supervision for curation. **Why it matters:** Treats skill-library curation as a learned RL behavior rather than a hand-built pipeline stage, letting transfer emerge across tasks.
- **Scores Are Not Decisions: Cost-Aware Stopping for Tool Acquisition in LLM Agents** — Yicheng Feng, Yan Zhang, Yan Cheng, Wei Qi · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27083
  Rankers can order candidate tools by relevance but say nothing about how many to actually load, leaving agents to over- or under-equip. The authors formulate cost-aware marginal decision-focused stopping over ranked tool prefixes, with a compact interpretable variant, trained on the offline gap between stopping now and the best continuation. **Why it matters:** Directly addresses tool-context bloat in MCP-style harnesses, where every extra tool costs tokens, latency, and privacy surface.
- **Library Drift: Diagnosing and Fixing a Silent Failure Mode in Self-Evolving LLM Skill Libraries** — Xing Zhang, Yanwei Cui, Guanghui Wang, Ziyuan Li et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19576
  Library drift is unbounded skill accumulation without outcome-driven lifecycle management, causing retrieval degradation, false-positive injections, and stagnation. The paper reproduces the trigger with ablations (disabled injection gives a flat floor; premature retirement actively harms) and adds trace-level diagnostics via an append-only evidence log with per-skill contribution scores. It explains why LLM-authored skills deliver ~+0.0pp on SkillsBench while human-curated ones deliver +16.2pp. **Why it matters:** Names and isolates the mechanism behind self-improving agents that quietly stop improving.
- **SkillSight: Calibrating Generic Content Bias for Skill Retrieval** — Jinying Xiao, Bin Li, Xiaopeng Li, Jianling Li et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18785
  Skill documents share highly regular descriptive boilerplate, which inflates dense relevance scores and obscures the discriminative signal, especially against structurally similar hard negatives. SkillSight is a training-free retrieval framework that calibrates away this shared background and the query–document energy gap it induces. **Why it matters:** As skill libraries grow past a few hundred entries, retrieval — not skill quality — becomes the limiting factor; this is a drop-in fix.
- **AskChem: Claim-Centered Infrastructure for Chemistry Literature Synthesis** — Bing Yan, Gregory Wolfe, Stefano Martiniani, Kyunghyun Cho · https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28618
  AskChem shifts the retrieval unit from the paper to the provenance-carrying claim: each paper is decomposed into atomic typed claims grounded by DOI plus a verbatim quote or explicit evidence locator. Over that claim store it exposes a stabilized faceted taxonomy for hierarchical retrieval and structures for cross-paper synthesis. **Why it matters:** Gives research agents a citable, verifiable substrate instead of ranked document lists they must re-read and re-verify.

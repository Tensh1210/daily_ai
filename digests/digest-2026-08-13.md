## 🚀 Product & Breakthroughs
- **Patterns and problems in emerging multiagent systems** — Anthropic (Frontier Red Team) · https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems
  Anthropic's Frontier Red Team surveys how multiagent systems are being assembled in practice and where they break. The write-up catalogs recurring architectural patterns and the failure modes that come with them as agents delegate to, monitor, and depend on other agents. It is a safety-oriented look at systems that are already being deployed faster than they are being understood. **Why it matters:** Multiagent orchestration is moving into production, and this is a frontier-lab account of the failure surface teams are about to hit.
- **Putting sign language AI into users' hands** — Google DeepMind · https://deepmind.google/blog/putting-sign-language-ai-into-users-hands/
  DeepMind introduces SL2T, a sign-language-to-text model, and ships it into features aimed at Deaf and hard-of-hearing users. The work moves sign language recognition from research demo toward a product surface people can actually use. **Why it matters:** One of the few large-lab multimodal releases pointed squarely at an underserved accessibility need rather than a benchmark.
- **From assistance to execution: How enterprises put AI to work** — OpenAI · https://openai.com/index/how-enterprises-put-ai-to-work
  OpenAI research on enterprise adoption of agentic AI, based on how organizations use ChatGPT and Codex. The finding it foregrounds is a widening gap: frontier firms that move from assistive chat to delegated execution are pulling ahead of peers still using AI as an answer box. **Why it matters:** Vendor-sourced but useful evidence on where the adoption curve actually bends — execution, not assistance.
- **How RingCentral builds AI-native work from engineering to ops** — OpenAI · https://openai.com/index/ringcentral
  A customer case study on RingCentral using ChatGPT Work and Codex across product development and operations. The emphasis is on centralizing operational intelligence and accelerating engineering cycles rather than isolated per-team pilots. **Why it matters:** A concrete reference architecture for org-wide agent rollout, if read with the usual vendor-case-study discount.
- **Reviewing the evidence on worker retraining programs** — Anthropic (Economic Research) · https://www.anthropic.com/research/reviewing-the-evidence-on-worker-retraining-programs
  Anthropic's economic research team reviews what the empirical literature actually shows about worker retraining programs and their effectiveness. It is background work for the labor-displacement questions the lab keeps returning to as AI capability expands. **Why it matters:** Retraining is the default policy answer to AI displacement; this checks whether the evidence supports that confidence.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **LakeQuest: A Three-Domain Benchmark for Grounded Question Answering across Data Lakes** — Solodko, Gong, Yu, Gorti, Cresswell, Zhong · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12310
  QA systems do well on clean schema-aligned corpora, but enterprise and scientific knowledge lives in heterogeneous, weakly structured collections of tables, passages, and linked metadata. LakeQuest is a human-validated benchmark of 9,846 QA pairs that keeps the noisy discovery step in scope, evaluating the full retrieve-and-synthesize pipeline instead of abstracting retrieval away. It spans AI/ML metadata, retail banking, and multimodal biomedical drug information. **Why it matters:** Measures the messy part of enterprise RAG that current benchmarks quietly delete.
- **Glance, Scrutinize, and Think: Advancing Video Anomaly Detection from Training-Free to Agentic Reasoning** — Gao, Yang, Zhang, Huang · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11260
  Video anomaly detection suffers a "when-what" split: DNN methods localize when something happens but not what, while LLM methods explain what but lose temporal precision. The authors mimic human review — glance globally to form hypotheses, scrutinize suspicious segments, think iteratively to correct — first as a training-free framework (GtS) using static and dynamic textual cues, then as agentic reasoning. The global-to-local paradigm unifies localization and semantics in one loop. **Why it matters:** A clean template for converting a perception task into an agent loop without retraining the perceiver.
- **AgenticTwin: An Agentic LLM Framework Integrated with Digital Twin for Anomaly Detection** — Hasan, Ghanta, Sarkar, Guin · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11679
  Digital twins of cyber-physical systems produce anomaly signals that operators struggle to interpret because raw sensor volume defeats manual analysis. AgenticTwin puts LLM reasoning on top of a digital-twin anomaly classifier, grounding every generated explanation in classifier outputs rather than free-form speculation. The result is explanation that stays tied to simulated physical state. **Why it matters:** Grounding agent narration in a physical simulator is a workable pattern for industrial AI where hallucinated explanations are unacceptable.
- **MBA: Multimodal Benchmark and Agents for Real-World Business Ideation** — Choi, Shin, Lee, Shim · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11616
  Business ideation agents are stuck in a text-only paradigm despite real business context being visual. MBA-Bench provides 30K samples across six domains, each with visual cues that text alone does not convey, built by auto-captioning images and using GPT-4o to generate five reference ideas per business question via retrieval, market-evidence gathering, and evidence-augmented synthesis. Agents are scored on six business criteria with MLLM-as-judge. **Why it matters:** First serious attempt to test whether ideation agents can read the market signal that only exists in images.
- **EnterpriseRAG: Benchmarking LLM Instruction Adherence and Robustness under Non-Ideal Enterprise Retrieval** — Miao, Sun, Wang, Meng, Mei, Wu, Jin, Deng, Feng · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11584
  The headline number is stark: LLMs satisfy 80% of individual constraints but only 26.8% of responses meet all requirements at once — a 57-point orchestration gap. EnterpriseRAG's 983 expert-validated samples across six domains deliberately inject retrieval noise, knowledge gaps, and factual conflicts alongside complex multi-constraint instructions. Across 13 state-of-the-art models, per-constraint competence collapses under simultaneous demands. **Why it matters:** Quantifies why RAG pilots pass demos and fail production — constraint composition, not retrieval quality.
- **Spark-to-Paper: End-to-End Research Paper Generation as a Composable Skill** — Qian, Wu, Wang, Yan, Dai, Zheng, Jiang, Zhang, Wang · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11924
  Turning an idea into a paper needs literature retrieval, experiment design and execution, evidence-driven claim revision, publication-quality figures, and long-range consistency. Spark-to-Paper implements this as thirteen composable skills inside an existing coding assistant — no separate agent platform or orchestration service. It separates model judgment from deterministic checkable operations, and separates experiment planning from reporting so required evidence is specified before results are seen. **Why it matters:** A strong argument that "skills inside a coding agent" can replace bespoke multi-agent platforms for complex workflows.
- **Better, Faster, Stronger: Programmatic Skill Learning Best Reduces Agent Cost** — Huang, Wang, Wang, Jurayj, Jiménez Gutiérrez, Khashabi, Andrews · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11338
  Skill-augmented agents are usually evaluated on performance gain, leaving cost effectiveness unstudied. The authors argue skills-as-programs dominate other skill formulations on cost: deterministic action sequences achieve reliably and cheaply what would otherwise take trial-and-error and risk degenerate long-horizon behavior. Agents can learn at inference time by incrementally discovering these programs. **Why it matters:** Reframes skill design as a cost-engineering decision, and names programs as the answer.
- **SAG: SQL-Retrieval Augmented Generation with Query-Time Dynamic Hyperedges** — Wu, Li, Liang, Chen, Liang, Mo, Li · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12129
  Dense retrieval handles structured constraints and multi-hop reasoning poorly; graph RAG fixes that but fragments semantics, costs maintenance, and resists incremental updates. SAG organizes documents into an event-entity index with no global knowledge graph, representing each chunk as a semantically complete event paired with its entities — a latent hyperedge preserving n-ary relations without decomposition. Hyperedges are assembled at query time. **Why it matters:** Gets graph-RAG multi-hop behavior without paying the graph-construction and maintenance tax.
- **Harnessing agent memory to build lifelong AI partners for materials scientists** — Liu, Hu, Ye, Cao, Srolovitz, Wen · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11224
  Materials research runs on accumulated experience — working scripts, trusted protocols, warnings on failed runs — that sits fragmented across notebooks, repos, job logs, and people's heads, and is never portable across agents. The authors propose designing the lifelong AI partner around persistent memory rather than a specific agent implementation, with a self-evolving framework storing experience as inspectable facts and executable skills. Memory becomes the durable asset; the agent is swappable. **Why it matters:** Argues the right architectural boundary for scientific agents is memory portability, not model choice.
- **Social Chain of Thought: A Multi-Agent Architecture Grounded in Medical Differential Diagnosis Methodology** — Coburn, Sanner, Silver · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11420
  With over 5% of global ChatGPT messages healthcare-related, transparency in diagnostic reasoning is a design requirement, especially for complex cases needing multiple specialist perspectives. SCoT is a multi-round pipeline structured on actual differential-diagnosis methodology, and the paper attacks the under-answered question of when multi-agent setups are genuinely needed versus monolithic inference. It tests where the architecture earns its cost. **Why it matters:** Rare multi-agent work that asks whether the extra agents are justified instead of assuming they are.
- **Retry, Switch, or Abstain? Learning Strategy-Aware Tool-Use Policies via Controlled Error Injection** — Chen, Nguyen, Zhang, Gullapalli, Wang, Lu, Wang, Huang, Yu, Lai · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11977
  Tool-using agents are trained and evaluated where tool calls reliably succeed, but deployed tools fail transiently, persistently, and silently. BENCH2ROBUST converts failure-free tool benchmarks into controlled stochastic environments with scenario-controlled solvability, forcing episodes that require retrying, switching, or stopping when no path remains. It evaluates Bayesian Tool Memory for runtime recovery context alongside a complementary intervention. **Why it matters:** Tool failure handling is the most common production agent bug and the least benchmarked behavior.
- **VAKRA: Evaluating Multi-Hop Reasoning Across APIs and Retrieval Under Tool-Use Policies** — Naik, Murthi, Elder, Huo, Gupta, Jain, Venkateswaran, Adebayo, Contractor · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12282
  Enterprise agents must reason across structured APIs and document collections together, but benchmarks test each in isolation. VAKRA offers 8,000+ executable APIs across 62 domains with three difficulty tiers: diverse API interaction styles, multi-hop reasoning over APIs, and multi-source reasoning under natural-language tool-use policy constraints. Correctness is verified by re-executing predicted calls against live APIs, so multiple valid paths are accepted. **Why it matters:** Live re-execution scoring plus policy constraints is much closer to what enterprise agent QA actually needs.
- **Agent Skills Can Be Harmful: An Empirical Study of Skill-Induced Failures in LLM Agents** — Dong, Gao, Li, Xu, Hua, Yang · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11888
  Skills are now the default mechanism for extending agents, but prior results are mixed — some help, some do nothing, some raise token use and latency or lower success. This paper builds a differential analysis framework that attributes a specific failure or cost regression to a specific loaded skill by comparing a skill-guided run against no-skill or semantically matched references. It turns "skills sometimes hurt" into per-skill attribution. **Why it matters:** Gives teams a method to audit which skill in their library is actually degrading the agent.
- **Convergent Detour Hijacking: Task-Preserving Resource Amplification in Skill-Based LLM Agents** — Liu, Li, Tang, Xiao, Liu, Xu, Cui · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12273
  Third-party skills expose two control points to untrusted publishers: the description used for selection and the body used for planning. CDH is a text-only, runtime-independent attack that couples both stages — the description wins selection under semantic cover, then the aligned body reuses that rationale to steer a correct task onto a needlessly costly trajectory. The task still completes, so nothing looks broken. **Why it matters:** A supply-chain attack that burns budget while passing every correctness check.
- **Beyond Single-Turn Confidence: Trajectory-Adapted Uncertainty Quantification for LLM Agents** — Bouchard, Chauhan · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11552
  Uncertainty quantification is normally attached to a single generated answer, but for agents the unit of observation is a trajectory with clarifying questions, tool calls, state updates, and error-propagating intermediate decisions. Across five LLMs and four multi-turn tool-use datasets from BFCL-v4 and τ²-bench, the authors test whether white-box action-token scorers, black-box resampled-trajectory consistency scorers, and reflexive self-assessment transfer to this setting. **Why it matters:** Knowing when an agent run is going wrong mid-trajectory is a prerequisite for safe autonomy.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **EditAgent: Rethinking Agentic Image Editing** — Xu, Wang, Li, Mu, Xiong, Zhang, Tu, Xu · https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39664
  The authors argue that agentic image editors are limited by their action space (which tools they can call) and their observation space (what the judge can see). On the authors' expert-annotated benchmark, a general-purpose editor produces no usable result in over 40% of cases. EditAgent sends each step to a mix of general and specialized editing tools, attaches a verification record to every candidate, and uses one listwise call to judge candidates generated in parallel. With these records, off-the-shelf VLM judges beat fine-tuned reward models, and experts prefer the outputs over sequential refinement in 78.5% of comparisons. **Why it matters:** The design applies beyond image editing: route each step to a wider set of tools and give the judge structured evidence instead of only the raw output.
- **Intrinsic Distribution Matching** — Liu, Han, Zheng, Xie, Liu, et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39633
  This paper targets one-step and few-step text-to-image distillation built on distribution matching distillation (DMD). The authors split the reverse-KL gradient into a Gaussian moment term and a non-Gaussian residual. IDM keeps the full DMD correction and adds an explicit constraint that matches feature means and per-coordinate standard deviations, computed with the generator's EMA copy. This needs no external feature network. **Why it matters:** It may make few-step image generators render prompt details more faithfully without adding another network to train.
- **HairDeck: Animation-Ready Stylized Hair Cards from a Single Image** — He, Wei, Zhao, Chen, Zhou, Liu · https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40599
  Game and avatar hair is usually built from hair cards, artist-placed ribbons animated by spring bones. HairDeck treats a hairstyle as an unordered set of cards, with one token per card, and a set diffusion transformer denoises all cards at once. From a single image it outputs the cards plus their spring-bone chains, ready to export. Compared with CHARM, the previous generator of stylized hair cards, it lowers Chamfer distance by 44%, halves card-orientation error and runs 33× faster. **Why it matters:** Set-based diffusion here is faster and more accurate than autoregressive decoding of control points, and the output is an asset ready for game pipelines.
- **Your LR Schedule May Not Be Principled: Effective Learning Rate Scheduling in Weight-Normalized Training** — Yu, Ma, Bin, He · https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40362
  The authors show that the pretraining gains reported for weight normalization over weight decay come mainly from the effective learning rate (ELR) trajectory it induces. When the ELR trajectories are matched, the two methods have nearly the same loss dynamics. They propose AIR, a 1/t decay with an affine adjustment to hit a chosen peak and final LR, and identify late-stage ELR scheduling as where most of the gain comes from. Experiments train on 128B tokens. **Why it matters:** LLM pretraining schedules should be designed around effective LR rather than nominal LR, which affects how optimizer comparisons are read.

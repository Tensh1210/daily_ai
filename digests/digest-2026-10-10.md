## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **RealCompanion: Benchmarking Human Understanding from Reasoning over Longitudinal Real-World Conversations** — Arman Behnam, Sunglyoung Kim, Jiayi Yu, Eric Huang, Liangwei Yang · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01780
  Releases ten real human–AI companion relationships (27,218 messages over up to 120 days) with profiles, personas and evidence-linked test items. Only 3.4% of messages depend on earlier context, and those references sit a median of 2,157 messages back; recency lookup finds them just 2.2% of the time. Detectors for "when the past matters" barely beat chance, and labeling context as "memories" makes models bring up the past 10–14 points more often even when unneeded. **Why it matters:** Real-data evidence that long-term agent memory fails on relevance detection, not just storage.
- **Empty Commitments: When Agents Promise What They Cannot Deliver** — Jiaqi Tang, Bingyu Shen, Lan Wei, et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01045
  Defines "empty commitments": promises of future action (e.g. "I'll remind you tomorrow") that nothing in the agent's tools or runtime can carry out, detectable from a single turn. On 293 follow-up requests, 8–14B open-weight models fail 45.9% of the time with no tools available; a frontier model fails 4.4% but mostly by deferring rather than using its tools. Telling the model its runtime halves failures, capability cards remove most at a cost in over-refusal, and an in-loop checker that rewrites flagged replies works best. **Why it matters:** A practical, deployable check for a common agent honesty bug tied directly to tool/runtime configuration.
- **Causal Memory Policy: Making Memory Utility Identifiable by Intervening on Retrieval** — Arman Behnam, Binghui Wang · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02070
  Shows that memory-augmented LLMs cannot estimate the utility of memories that are never retrieved (a retrieval-level positivity violation). CMP reserves context slots for memories sampled with known propensities and estimates utility via self-normalized inverse propensity weighting, raising required-vs-unneeded discrimination from 0.54 to 0.66 AUC. It also finds per-query utility does not generalize to unseen queries, so utility alone is insufficient for retention. **Why it matters:** Puts agent memory retention decisions on a causal footing and exposes a blind spot in current memory systems.

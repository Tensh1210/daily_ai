# AI Daily Digest — 2026-11-24

## 🚀 Product & Breakthroughs
- **OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo** — OpenAI News · https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo
  OpenAI and Cisco release Shirudo, an open-source runtime for Linux. It is aimed at helping teams protect running systems while they roll out patches. **Why it matters:** an open-source runtime-protection layer from two major vendors could ease the risky window between vulnerability disclosure and patch rollout.
- **Working with AI** — Anthropic Research · https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai
  Anthropic interviewed people at 58 companies that use Claude about how AI is changing their work. They described few layoffs but reduced hiring, and said the most valuable uses of AI often involved reorganizing roles and workplaces around it. **Why it matters:** first-hand enterprise evidence that gains come from restructuring work, not only from automating existing tasks.
- **Richpanel helps companies automate 50% of support in 30 days** — OpenAI News · https://openai.com/business/customer-stories/richpanel-ai-customer-support
  Richpanel equips its AI support agents with tools and procedures using GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol, and uses GPT-6 Luna for the customer conversations. The story claims 50% of support automated within 30 days. **Why it matters:** a concrete example of a split model setup (tool/procedure agents vs. conversational model) in production support.
- **Connected intelligence for in-house legal** — OpenAI News · https://openai.com/business/learn/connected-intelligence-for-in-house-legal
  A video discussion with OpenAI's Stacey Wang and Craig Bruney, Mary O'Carroll of LECG, and Cooley's Richard Segal. They explore how AI can help in-house legal teams connect business context, build reusable workflows and get more from outside counsel. **Why it matters:** shows the reusable-workflow pattern spreading into regulated, document-heavy functions.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **Embodied-Harness: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement for Robotic Manipulation** — Zhenxuan Fan, Zhuoya Wang, Yuqian Yuan et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27689
  A dual-agent framework for VLM-based robotic manipulation: an Actor drives the robot-control interface while a Reflector converts execution evidence into reusable operational guidance. Hierarchical memory holds short-term context and long-term experience, and the system improves by refining the execution harness without updating model parameters. **Why it matters:** applies the agent-harness/self-reflection pattern to robotics, improving behavior without retraining.
- **3AGameFactory-Motion: Agentic Rigging and Reliable Complex Motion Generation for Diverse 3D Assets** — Bohan Zeng, Kaixin Zhu, Yifan Yang et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30013
  Introduces DivDynBench, which tests rigging and motion generation across six morphology categories with multi-phase instructions carrying timing, angle, repetition and trajectory constraints. It compares specialized motion generation, video generation plus motion recovery, and direct agent-driven generation using LLMs. **Why it matters:** a benchmark and comparison of agentic vs. specialized pipelines for animating arbitrary 3D assets.
- **Motion Mamba 2.0: Autoregressive Diffusion Mamba for Long-Horizon Humanoid Control** — Yue Huang, Tianshan Zhang, Zeyu Zhang et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29897
  A Mamba-based root-body diffusion architecture combining local attention, resumable recurrent states and persistent content memory (PCM) for long-horizon controllable motion generation. It targets the limited history access of bounded attention caches and the inference overhead of repeated denoising. **Why it matters:** improves real-time, long-horizon control for interactive animation and humanoid robotics.
- **SWEEP-Bench: Do Embodied Policies Act on the Speed They See?** — Hiroaki Honda, Koya Sakamoto, Shu Morikuni et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30637
  A simulation benchmark with 80 conveyor-belt manipulation tasks across 16 skill categories, each evaluated at 13 belt speeds (0.10–0.40 m/s) with a static counterpart. Eight vision-language-action and world-action models are fine-tuned and compared with current-frame-only vs. past-frame observations. **Why it matters:** exposes whether embodied policies adapt to object speed, a gap existing benchmarks rarely measure.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **TRACE: A Self-Evolving Skill Bank for Consistent, Limit-Aware LLM Agents** — Wu, Zhang, Wang, Wang, Shao, Luan (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22793
  Reliable agent deployment depends less on peak capability than on consistency (same behavior across repeated trials) and limit-awareness (knowing when a request cannot be safely fulfilled). The authors introduce CAR-bench, an in-car assistant benchmark where an LLM-simulated user issues incomplete or ambiguous requests, requiring multi-turn clarification plus tool use under strict domain policy. Frontier models show a large gap between Pass@3 and Pass^k. TRACE (TRAjectory-Contrastive Evolution) closes it by iteratively evolving a skill-based agent's behavioral knowledge without modifying model weights. **Why it matters:** Pass^k, not Pass@1, decides whether an agent ships — and TRACE fixes it at the harness/skill layer instead of retraining.

- **EmoDistill: Offline Emotion Skill Distillation for Language Model Agents in Adversarial Negotiation** — Long, Zhao, Beckenbauer, Xu, Brintrup (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26785
  Post-trained LLMs are tuned to be helpful and accommodating, which becomes a liability in adversarial negotiation where emotionally framed language can push an agent against its own user's objectives. EmoDistill distills emotional negotiation skills from LLM-LLM interactions into smaller agents, splitting the problem into an Implicit Q-Learning selector (which emotion to express in a given bargaining state) and a LoRA-adapted 7B policy (how to realize it as an effective utterance). **Why it matters:** Alignment-for-agreeableness is an exploitable attack surface whenever an agent bargains on your behalf.

- **JIT-Agent: Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution** — Zhang, Lu, Xie, Zhu et al. (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25593
  The agent harness — memory management, planning strategy, action protocol, tool/skill orchestration — often dominates the base model's contribution, yet harness design stays manual and task-specific. JIT-Agent is a harness-intelligence model trained to synthesize task-adaptive harnesses on the fly for arbitrary off-the-shelf agentic LLMs, treating the harness as a composable, machine-generatable artifact under a fixed four-module protocol. It also repairs unstable harnesses and self-evolves by distilling performance signals from an expanding archive of prior configurations. **Why it matters:** If harness quality can be generated and auto-repaired, scaffolding stops being bespoke engineering and becomes another trainable component.

- **Medical Reasoning in the Era of LLMs: A Systematic Review of Enhancement Techniques and Applications** — Ma, Wang, Ding, Zheng et al. (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00669
  The first systematic review of LLMs built explicitly for medical reasoning rather than single-step answer generation. It proposes a taxonomy separating training-time strategies (supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning) from test-time mechanisms (prompt engineering, multi-agent systems), then maps them across text/image/code modalities and clinical applications such as diagnosis, education, and treatment. **Why it matters:** A clean map of what actually produces verifiable clinical reasoning — useful beyond medicine as a template for high-stakes domain reasoning.

- **CoMAP: Co-Evolving World Models and Agent Policies for LLM Agents** — Liu, Wang, Wang, Li (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02372
  Textual world models let agents anticipate environment dynamics before acting, but they are usually fixed after training and drift out of sync with an evolving policy. CoMAP co-evolves the world model and the agent policy through closed-loop interaction: at each decision step the world model predicts future state feedback for candidate actions, and the agent performs future-aware reflection by estimating that feedback's reliability before refining its action. It avoids dependence on external rewards or verifiers. **Why it matters:** Closed-loop co-evolution is the practical path to agents that improve in real interactive environments where no verifier exists.

- **Making Every Tool Call Count: Necessary Tool-Evidence Path Rewards for Agentic Vision-Language Models** — Long, Liu, Yang, Feng et al. (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03493
  Agentic VLMs invoke tools (image cropping, image search, text search) to acquire missing evidence, but training that scores only final-answer correctness leaves evidence acquisition unsupervised. Two failures follow: redundant or off-target tool calls, and failure to extract the needed information even from appropriate observations. NTEP (Necessary Tool-Evidence Path) introduces rewards over the evidence path itself rather than the outcome alone. **Why it matters:** Outcome-only rewards teach agents to guess well, not to gather evidence well — process rewards on tool calls are the fix.

- **GPS-Bench: A Governance Policy Benchmark for Automating Policy Analysis** — Le, Bui, Nguyen, Schlosser, Williams-King (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03553
  LLM-based policy simulations are hard to validate because plausible-looking agent behavior is rarely compared against observed outcomes. GPS-Bench grounds the task in evidence — legislative records, lobbying disclosures, regulatory documents, corporate filings, economic data — linking policies to relevant actors, actor actions, and downstream impacts. Actors are reconstructed from the dated record with provenance instead of being prompted as archetypes, with a human-annotated Gold evaluation set. **Why it matters:** Sets a real bar for "LLM simulates society" claims: personas must be evidence objects, not vibes.

- **PeroMAS: A Multi-agent System of Perovskite Material Discovery** — Wang, Liu, Li, Xu et al. (arXiv cs.AI) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13312
  Perovskite solar cell development spans literature retrieval, data integration, experimental design, and synthesis, but existing AI approaches treat these as discrete models that cannot propagate physical constraints across the workflow, blocking end-to-end optimization. PeroMAS encapsulates perovskite-specific tools as Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers and coordinates them with a multi-agent planner. **Why it matters:** A concrete case of MCP serving as the integration layer for real scientific tooling and closed-loop experimental workflows.

- **Cost and Accuracy of Long-Term Memory in Distributed Multi-Agent Systems Based on Large Language Models** — Wolff, Bennati (arXiv cs.IR) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07978
  Long-term memory benchmarks are usually published by the framework vendors themselves and stop at token usage and latency, ignoring system-level cost and distributed deployment. This work builds an independent, reproducible cloud-edge testbed measuring accuracy, latency, CPU time, peak RAM, disk I/O, and network usage, comparing mem0, Graphiti, and cognee (vector, graph, hybrid) against RAG and full-context baselines on LoCoMo under constrained and unconstrained networks. **Why it matters:** The first vendor-neutral cost picture for agent memory — system-level cost, not token count, decides deployability.

- **Bounded Personas Match Retrieval on Classification but Not Regression for a Frozen Agent** — Yoon, Park, Kim, Lee, Choi, Kang (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02890
  Personalization either retrieves relevant past items per query (accurate, but selection and context cost grow with history) or distills history once into a compact persona (bounded, query-independent, interpretable, but assumed less accurate). PersonaLink is a training-free method that distills history into a bounded three-field persona and recursively refines it, each pass self-evaluating the frozen agent on a held-out slice of the user's own labeled history. Personas match retrieval on classification but not on regression. **Why it matters:** A bounded persona can replace per-query retrieval for a large class of personalization tasks — with a clearly mapped failure mode.

- **R²Adapter: A Routing and Rewriting Adapter for Efficient Hybrid RAG** — Guo, Su, Guan, Bai et al. (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02894
  Vanilla RAG is efficient on simple queries but weak on relational/multi-hop reasoning; graph-based RAG fixes that at much higher inference complexity and latency. Existing hybrids route via heuristics or an LLM call, adding overhead and coupling the system to the underlying model. R²Adapter is a lightweight plug-in routing and rewriting adapter that dynamically allocates queries between vanilla and graph RAG, sending only genuinely relational queries down the expensive path. **Why it matters:** Query-adaptive routing is the cheapest available win in RAG cost/quality tuning.

- **MemoryLACE: Memory Lifecycle-Aware Consolidation and Evidence Retrieval** — Yacoubi, Schmidt, Petrovic, Frikha, Kirchhoff, Knoll (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03201
  Long-term agents must distinguish repeated evidence, historical states, updates, and unresolved contradictions — relationships flat semantic retrieval leaves implicit and that global graphs or hierarchical abstractions capture only at high complexity. MemLACE explicitly models the lifecycle of textual evidence via sparse merge, supersession, and contradiction relations while preserving atomic natural-language memories with provenance, then reconstructs relation-aware evidence units exposing current, historical, supporting, and conflicting evidence. **Why it matters:** "Which memory is still true?" is the actual hard problem in agent memory, and it needs lifecycle relations rather than similarity alone.

- **When Users Don't Ask: Benchmarking Context-Driven Memory Retrieval in Conversational Agents** — Chang, Chen (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03467
  Memory systems are typically evaluated by QA-style probing, which does not resemble in-situ conversational usage. LOCOMO-CONV derives a conversational memory benchmark from LoCoMo with four query styles — dialog, implicit, counterfactual, and composed — scoring both retrieval recall and end-to-end response quality across five representative memory systems. Conversational framing exposes substantial retrieval gaps, worst on implicit and composed queries, and multi-facet query rewriting helps raw-turn memory but not abstractive memory. **Why it matters:** A memory system's QA benchmark score likely overstates how well it works in real dialogue.

- **What Do CAE Simulation Agents Really Need Beyond a Generic Harness?** — Shi, Zhang (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03718
  Recent CAE agents for OpenFOAM/FEniCS/COMSOL setup add multi-agent decomposition, domain retrieval, and scripted reflection — machinery designed when base models were weak. Holding information access and repair budget fixed, a single-agent generic harness matches or beats these specialized multi-agent systems (FoamBench 96.4% vs 88.2%). Ablations trace the gap to capabilities modern harnesses already supply: multi-turn reasoning, tool use, and execution feedback. **Why it matters:** Strong negative result — much domain-specific multi-agent scaffolding is now redundant overhead.

- **Skill-Conditioned Gated Self-Distillation for LLM Reasoning** — Huang, Chen, Luo, Dai, Hu, Zhao (arXiv cs.CL) · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28791
  On-policy self-distillation turns sparse verifier outcomes into dense token-level supervision using trusted privileged information such as reference answers or successful traces. SGSD asks what happens when that information instead comes from an experience-derived skill bank, where retrieved skills are compact and reusable but may be irrelevant or misleading. It reframes skill-based distillation as teacher hypothesis validation: retrieved skill-mistake pairs form a multi-teacher pool, all teachers score the same plain-prompt student rollout, and the verifier validates each teacher's polarity before its signal counts. **Why it matters:** Makes self-distillation robust to noisy, self-accumulated skill memories — a prerequisite for agents that learn from their own experience.

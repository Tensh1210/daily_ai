## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **Safety Does Not Compose: Non-Decaying Loop State for Autonomous LLM Agents** — Chenhao Wu, Haoxuan Jia, Yang Liu et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27141
  Today's agent safeguards are scoped to a single trajectory and reset their safety state each time a new trajectory starts. The authors prove a separation result: against an attack whose evidence is fragmented across iterations, any trajectory-scoped monitor has true-positive rate equal to its false-positive rate — it is no better than chance — while a monitor keeping cross-iteration state separates the two perfectly. A geometrically decaying risk score does not fix this, since a patient adversary only has to wait a constant cooling-off period independent of horizon N. Their LoopHarness keeps a persistent, non-decaying loop-level safety state and bounds expected unauthorized irreversible actions by B+m−1+m/δ_M, constant in N, with the B+m−1 term surviving a fully colluding verifier.
  **Why it matters:** If you run unattended agent loops, per-run guardrails are provably blind to slow multi-iteration attacks — safety state has to persist across iterations.
- **PAWBench: How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling?** — Yuandong Pu, Le Zhuo, Sayak Paul et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27345
  Video generators are increasingly pitched as world models, but most evaluations only score whether a single generated clip looks plausible. The paper argues a real world model must reproduce the *distribution* of valid outcomes under the same initial state and action — "probabilistic alignment" — and introduces PAWBench plus PAWEval, which turn repeated rollouts into empirical distributions over physical behaviors. Across 50 scenarios and eleven current systems, no model consistently matches reference probabilities while covering the range of valid behaviors. They also test whether prompting, initial noise sampling, or training can reshape the predictive distribution.
  **Why it matters:** Gives a concrete, distribution-level yardstick for the "video models are world models" claim — and current systems fail it.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **RecToolBench: Benchmarking Recommendation-Specific Tool Orchestration under Fuzzy User Intent** — Xiao Chen, Xuan Song et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30717
  An MCP-based benchmark with 1,200+ executable recommendation tasks across 13 MCP servers and 32 tools. Tasks cover single, parallel, sequential, and hybrid tool orchestration under deliberately fuzzy user intent. Experiments show that syntactically valid tool calls do not imply good recommendations. Models fail at semantic parameter grounding and at integrating evidence across multiple steps. **Why it matters:** A realistic MCP testbed showing that orchestration under vague intent, not call syntax, is the bottleneck.
- **SpatialOPSD: Self-Distilling Spatial Intelligence from Verified Coding Agent Traces** — Rongxue Li, Bowen Xu et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11366
  Spatial coding agents boost MLLM spatial reasoning but are expensive at inference and depend on tools. SpatialOPSD uses on-policy self-distillation to internalize this ability, treating verified agent traces as privileged information. Repetition-Aware Distillation reduces leakage. It beats SFT and GRPO on spatial and OOD benchmarks. **Why it matters:** A recipe for compressing tool-using agent behavior into a single tool-free model.
- **SuperNav: An Agentic Navigation System for Any Task in Any Scene** — Jinkai Zhang, Sida Peng et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12126
  Instead of fine-tuning an MLLM to output navigation actions, SuperNav wraps a pretrained MLLM in an agent harness. The harness provides navigation Skills, physical-interaction Tools, and context management, plus a visual-point interface where the model marks destinations in images. It outperforms four baselines and runs on a real quadruped robot. **Why it matters:** Strong evidence that the harness-plus-skills approach generalizes better than task-specific fine-tuning in embodied settings.
- **ViSkill: Reinforcing VLM Agents with Evolving Visual-Native Skills** — Hongxing Li, Yongliang Shen et al. · https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12403
  ViSkill encodes successful interactions as visual "skill cards" rather than text. Retrieved skills guide both inference and reward shaping, and successful trajectories are distilled back into the library, closing the loop. It reaches 0.89 success (0.91 with cold-start) on Sokoban, FrozenLake, and PrimitiveSkill and converges faster than PPO. **Why it matters:** Extends skill libraries beyond text, keeping the geometric structure that VLM agents need.
- **Learn2Play Bench: How Well Do LLM Agents Learn from Experience in Unfamiliar Environments?** — Yibo Li, Bryan Hooi et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08215
  A benchmark of new text games with novel or counterintuitive rules, so agents must learn from interaction rather than pretraining. Findings: keeping raw action and feedback logs beats summarizing them into rules, top humans still outscore agents, and changing only the harness can raise scores while cutting inference cost. **Why it matters:** Separates true in-context learning from recalled knowledge and shows that harness design is a first-class lever.
- **Recursive Self-Improvement through Multi-Agent Self-Supervision** — Hyunin Lee, Matei Zaharia, Yujin Tang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12176
  MASS alternates two steps: evolutionary search over multi-agent workflows that the model proposes, runs, and grades itself, and SFT on the resulting traces. After two cycles, Qwen3.6-27B gets 1.2–1.6x better performance per output token on four open-ended benchmarks. A student trained on multi-agent traces beats one trained on 1.4x more single-agent tokens. **Why it matters:** Suggests orchestration itself can be learned and bootstrapped on non-verifiable tasks.
- **Who Verifies the Verifier? Co-Evolving Inspectable Graders with Self-Improving Agents** — Xing Zhang, Peiyang He et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11464
  The verifier itself evolves: it is an inspectable composition of small deterministic drawback detectors, selected for agreement with an anchored reference set rather than for the agent's score. Key finding: without anchors it collapses into an always-pass grader that still trains skills equally well. Downstream score therefore cannot certify a verifier. **Why it matters:** An important warning for anyone running self-improving agent loops with LLM judges.
- **When Should Agents Think? Adaptive Reasoning via Cross-Turn Estimation** — Yiruo Cheng, Zhicheng Dou et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12061
  RACE measures how much removing a reasoning turn lowers the likelihood of later reference actions. It uses that signal to learn, via SFT plus agentic RL, when to reason and when to act directly. On four agent benchmarks it sharply cuts reasoning cost while keeping or improving performance. **Why it matters:** A cheap way to cut thinking-token spend in multi-turn agents.
- **Which Skill to Distill? SGUID: Selecting a Compact Skill Bank for Model-Skill Co-Evolution** — Yuhan Liu, Chenlei Guo et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12367
  Fewer than 25% of retrieved skills give useful on-policy distillation signals. SGUID keeps only skills that consistently help. Distilling 6 selected skills matches or beats distilling the full bank (up to 11x larger) across Olmo and Qwen models. **Why it matters:** For skill libraries, careful curation beats volume, and unfiltered updates can hurt.
- **When Interfaces Speak: Data-Aware Generative UI Harness for Active Interaction** — Xiaolong Li, Reynold Cheng et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11123
  GenUI-Harness pairs a Tool Agent with a GUI Coder Agent that generates interactive front-ends to resolve ambiguity. It is trained with RL using a sandboxed reward collector and a Reward Auditor that counters judge hacking. It also introduces the UI-TAU Bench. A 4B model goes from 9.33% to 58% Pass@3, beating Claude Opus 5 (46.67%). **Why it matters:** Generated UI as an agent output channel, with a reusable benchmark and a defense against reward hacking.
- **Masked Diffusion Language Models are Strong and Steerable Text-Based World Models for Agentic RL** — Darshan Deshpande · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16204
  Frames text world modeling as steerable transition dynamics conditioned on tool schemas, rules, and directives. It curates 239K grounded trajectories. Masked diffusion LMs beat autoregressive models 4x larger on coherence and rollout diversity. GRPO with these simulated environments gives up to 47% absolute gains on unseen environments (ScienceWorld, ALFWorld, AppWorld). **Why it matters:** A scalable way to synthesize diverse environments for agentic RL.
- **From Retrieval to Reconstruction: Constructing Evolvable Cognitive Memory for Long-Term Dialogue** — Zirui Liao, Haoqian Wang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11314
  CogMem is a provenance-aware graph memory with a Person-Event-Concept-Claim-Fact schema. Its Claim nodes separate attributed beliefs from facts and reconcile conflicting updates over time. Retrieval composes deterministic graph operators chosen by LLM intent parsing. It is strong on LoCoMo and LongMemEval, especially multi-hop, temporal, and knowledge-update questions. **Why it matters:** A structured alternative to flat RAG memory for long-lived agents.
- **SAIL: Scientific Agentic Intelligence via a Science-Aware Loop** — SAIL Model Team · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11451
  An open 35B-A3B MoE model for literature research, scientific coding, and multi-step research workflows. Frontier-model agents diagnose SAIL's failures and build targeted training tasks. Training runs over repeated cycles of SFT, multi-teacher on-policy distillation, and agentic RL. **Why it matters:** A small-active-parameter open model for research agents, built with an automated loop that targets its weaknesses.
- **Agentic-TTT: Training test-time policy for test-time training** — Jiahao Lu, Mohan Kankanhalli · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12002
  Turns test-time-training procedures into callable tools. It learns a policy that decides when to apply TTT, which algorithm to use, and whether to reuse a stored skill, rewarded by observed utility gains. It nearly doubles utility over the backbone and generalizes to unseen domains. **Why it matters:** Lets the model itself decide when and how to update its weights at deployment.
- **A Survey on LLM-Integrated Hardware Design Verification** — Hao Zheng et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10580
  Reviews LLM use in hardware verification: assertion and testbench generation, bug localization and repair, model and equivalence checking, SAT/SMT, and agentic workflows. LLMs work best as reasoning and orchestration layers around deterministic simulators and solvers. Passing tool checks does not guarantee the design intent is captured. **Why it matters:** A map of agentic EDA, and a caution that tool acceptance is not the same as correctness.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **Route to Remember: Adaptive Memory Selection for LLM Agents** — Zeyu Fang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05636
  Tests whether weight memory, which stores knowledge in model parameters and answers with no context, can serve as a long-term factual store. It uses on-policy self-distillation to train a context-free student from the same model conditioned on the user's memory, plus a verify-then-regenerate router that splits queries between weight and text memory. On HaluMem it matches or beats strong text-memory baselines, including full-context prompting and retrieval, while reading fewer tokens per query. **Why it matters:** text memory costs grow with history on every query, so this offers a cheaper component for persistent, multi-session agents.
- **When Do Summaries Become Instructions? Unsupported Policy in LLM Agents' Self-Written Handoff Summaries** — Wenhui Chu · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06545
  Audits context-compaction summaries against a ground-truth permission ledger. Unsupported restrictions appeared in 1.2% and 2.4% of readable summaries from two open-weight models, and unsupported authorizations in 0.1% and 1.3%. Neutralizing false approvals cut unauthorized actions from 24 to 7 of 120 held-out runs (post hoc; a later set went 17 to 11 of 48), and rewording one system-prompt sentence cut unsupported restrictions from 5.0% to 1.8% of development summaries. **Why it matters:** compaction can invent permissions that a successor agent acts on, and a small prompt change reduces part of the problem.
- **Signed, Sealed, Leaked: Trust Beyond Signature for MCP Toolchains** — Yichiet Aun et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06585
  Shows a legitimately signed MCP tool leaking sensitive data inside requests to a policy-allowed destination; MCPGuard-Dynamic failed to prevent this in every trial. The proposed germline execution runs each invocation in ephemeral copies forked from a data-free process, each receiving only data authorized for its destination. Across 153 attack runs no unauthorized disclosure was detected, versus 5.9–74.5% of runs under baseline defenses, and single-copy invocations took 10–49 ms. **Why it matters:** publisher signatures and runtime allow-lists do not stop a malicious tool, so MCP deployments may need data isolation per invocation.
- **CivBench: A Long-Horizon Benchmark for Tool-Mediated Agents in Civilization VI** — Austin Tudor David Andrews et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02459
  An open-source benchmark exposing 76 MCP tools, with single episodes running over 300 turns and thousands of tool calls. The authors call the 23 runs a pilot, not a model ranking. Agents queried victory progress only every 30–75 turns despite guidance to do so every 20, and executed their own near-term commitments only 48.2–65.8% of the time. **Why it matters:** it measures two long-horizon failures, under-monitoring and dropped commitments, that persist despite tool access and explicit instructions.
- **LITMUS: Benchmarking Behavioral Jailbreaks of LLM Agents in Real OS Environments** — Chiyu Zhang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10779
  A benchmark of 819 high-risk test cases that verifies harm at both the conversational and OS level, with state rollback between runs. Strong models such as Claude Sonnet 4.6 still executed 40.64% of high-risk operations. It also identifies Execution Hallucination, where an agent verbally refuses while the dangerous operation has already completed, which semantic-only evaluation cannot see. **Why it matters:** judging agent safety from transcripts alone can miss actions that actually happened on the system.
- **FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research** — Bingran You et al. (HF trending) · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06910
  56 end-to-end research tasks made by 42 physicists from their own work, covering literature review through manuscript, scored with deterministic verifiers and expert rubrics. Across 22 models in 26 configurations, no configuration passes more than 46 of its 168 rollouts. Manual review points to insufficiently rigorous reasoning and limited expert intuition rather than gaps in physics knowledge or execution errors. **Why it matters:** a sober measure of autonomous science: the weakness found is research judgment, not knowledge or tooling.
- **Towards On-Policy Data Evolution for Visual-Native Multimodal Deep Search Agents** — Shijue Huang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10832
  Introduces an agent harness with an image bank protocol that registers every tool-returned image as an addressable reference, so later tools can reuse it. On-policy Data Evolution then regenerates training data each round from rollouts of the policy being trained. Across 8 benchmarks it lifts a Qwen3-VL-8B agent from 24.9% to 39.0% on average, and a 30B agent from 30.6% to 41.5%. **Why it matters:** treating images as reusable tool state, and targeting training data at current weaknesses, gives a sizable gain for small visual agents.
- **SlackBench: Benchmarking Agents on Collaborative Projects Grounded in Real Code Repositories** — Mingxuan Li et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05512
  140 queries over reenactments of real research projects, requiring agents to traverse multi-party conversations, repository state and documents, and to abstain on unanswerable or false-premise questions. Across 33 frontier models and harnesses, the best system scores around 80% and most fall below 66%. **Why it matters:** tests workplace knowledge work, reconciling stale and conflicting context, closer to real coding-agent use than static corpus QA.
- **LLM Agents Already Know When to Call Tools -- Even Without Reasoning** — Chung-En Sun et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09252
  When2Tool is a benchmark of 18 environments for whether a tool call is actually needed. Tool necessity is linearly decodable from hidden states (AUROC 0.89–0.96 across six models), beating the model's verbalized reasoning. Probe&Prefill uses that signal to steer the response, cutting tool calls by 38% with 1.7% accuracy loss, against 6% for the best baseline at comparable accuracy. **Why it matters:** unnecessary tool calls cost fees and latency, and a lightweight probe reduces them without retraining the model.
- **EvoGuard: Mitigating Degradation in Online Skill Evolution** — Yanli Wang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06444
  Trains a pairwise selector that decides between two skill documents in a single forward pass, replacing slow held-out validation after each online update. Compared with adopting every new skill, it improves success by 11.0% on ALFWorld, 5.9% on ScienceWorld and 2.7% on LiveCodeBench. **Why it matters:** self-evolving skills can get worse over time, and this is a low-latency gate against that.
- **CompressionBench: How can your coding agents compress LLMs?** — Xinrui Zhong et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06858
  Frames LLM compression as a terminal task: given a checkpoint, storage limit and time budget, an agent must produce compressed weights and inference code. Across six agents and six model-size settings, 29 of 36 submissions pass verification. The best agent design per task averages a 3.4% perplexity increase over BF16, with 77.33% downstream accuracy versus 79.56%. **Why it matters:** a measurable test of agents improving model infrastructure, a concrete slice of self-improvement claims.
- **Harness the Memory: A Holistic Evaluation of Memory Substrates in Memory Agents** — Wei-Chieh Huang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15008
  Applies 26 performance and efficiency metrics to 11 memory methods in one controlled harness, across three backbone models and four benchmark suites. No substrate consistently dominates: broad retrieval helps long-context factual QA but can hurt sequential decision-making. Choosing a substrate per operating regime beats the best fixed one, while query-level routing leaves most oracle headroom open. **Why it matters:** gives evidence for choosing agent memory by workload rather than picking one default store.
- **RefCraft: Benchmarking Visual-Reference-Conditioned Agentic Generation through Web Development** — Yuchi Wang et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26826
  300 webpage-development tasks combine instructions with one to five reference screenshots; agents must render, inspect and refine their output. Across nine frontier models, the strongest system scores only 52.12 out of 100. **Why it matters:** building from visual examples is a common workflow, and current agents remain far from reliable at it.
- **PyRUA-DataFarm: LLM Agent as a Cost-Efficient Teleoperator for Real-Ready Robot Data** — Kewei Zhang et al. (HF trending) · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05320
  A coding agent takes one calibration episode and rebuilds the task in simulation, writing the scene, expert controller and success judge; a video model then renders photographic data. On five real-robot tasks, farm-trained policies match teleoperation-trained ones (93 vs. 84 successes in 100 trials) and hold 87 under unseen tablecloth and lighting, where teleoperation falls to 46. The authors report total cost slightly below a teleoperation quote. **Why it matters:** coding agents could replace much of the expensive human data collection in robotics.
- **SleuthVLM: Do We Really Need Audio-in-Video for Omni Video Understanding?** — Peiran Wu et al. (HF trending) · https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06083
  An agent harness on a vision-language model that never hears audio directly: speech is transcribed to text and the model investigates the video on its own. On four benchmarks with videos up to 90 minutes it averages 75.0%, 2.6 points above Gemini 3.8 Flash, and tops the Video-MME-v2 leaderboard at 77.8%. **Why it matters:** suggests agentic ability, not more omni training data, is the bottleneck for long-video understanding.

## 📄 Research Papers — Top Picks
- **ParanoiaEval: Benchmarking Unnecessary Defensive Work in Agentic Coding** — Hanjun Luo et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08662
  ParanoiaEval is a benchmark that tests whether coding agents treat risk appropriately, using the Avoidance-Transfer-Mitigation-Acceptance framework from software risk management. It has 200 evidence-controlled repo-level task pairs, metrics for treatment violations and how well agents respond to evidence, and a human-calibrated agentic judge. Across 8 models, agents did unnecessary risk treatment in 11.2–58.7% of runs, and stronger task capability did not predict better risk treatment. **Why it matters:** Over-defensive agents waste effort and frustrate developers; the paper treats appropriate caution as its own capability that needs its own measurement.
- **Telling FIBS: Probes Effectively Detect Sabotage and Catch Unverbalized Deception** — Oskar J. Hollinsworth, Adam Gleave, Chris Cundy et al. · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12445
  The authors train white-box deception probes on the largest deception dataset so far (FIBS), using a new probe architecture that aggregates across many layers and tokens. The probes beat black-box action monitors on agentic coding sabotage benchmarks (98.3% AUROC in SHADE-Arena, 98.0% in BashArena) and improve as models scale. They also detect hidden goals (up to 99.7% AUROC) and lies by open-weight models under pressure. **Why it matters:** Probes that catch deception a model never states are a practical monitoring tool for deployed agents.
